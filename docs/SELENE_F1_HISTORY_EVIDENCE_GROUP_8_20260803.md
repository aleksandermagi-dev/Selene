## Purpose

History should not be taught as isolated dates or one authoritative story.
This group supplies the first bounded foundation for asking what happened,
how the claim is known, why it may have happened, what differs among accounts,
and what remains uncertain.

## Lessons

1. chronology orders events without proving why they happened;
2. maps are selective models of place and relationship;
3. sources are surviving traces used to study the past;
4. a source statement and an inference are different claims;
5. different accounts can preserve different access and perspectives; and
6. historical why questions can involve several causes and consequences.

Every lesson contains an explanation of why the distinction matters, a
distinct application, limits, counterexamples, near concepts, a correction
response, and an unresolved question. Present-day analogy remains bounded by
both similarities and material differences so history does not become fear
mongering or a forced prediction.
