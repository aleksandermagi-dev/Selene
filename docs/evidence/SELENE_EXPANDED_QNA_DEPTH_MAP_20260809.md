# Selene Expanded Q&A Depth Map

Date: 2026-08-09
Installed application sessions: 187, 188, 189, and 190
Status: observations recorded; no repair pass performed

## Purpose

The first post-reinstall Q&A found that NLO often received the wrong content or
no answer substance. Three additional conversations deliberately changed the
subjects, wording, dialogue shapes, and social situations to determine how deep
that integration problem runs.

This was not a repeated benchmark script. The added sessions covered:

- bridges, bones, school scheduling, structural analogy, counterexamples, and
  reversible trials;
- ordinary sharing, correction, apology, disagreement, sarcasm, mixed emotion,
  and conversational restraint; and
- a yard stone, observation versus inference, shadows, heat, a wobbly shelf,
  practical judgment, humor, ambiguous praise, thread returns, and a
  three-topic summary.

The tests remained ordinary and non-adversarial. Findings describe current
implementation behavior, not Selene's worth, identity, personality, or effort.

## Calibration correction

A later controlled comparison separated questions backed by approved F1
knowledge from questions clearly beyond the current curriculum. See
[F1 Knowledge Versus Graceful-Fall Q&A](SELENE_F1_KNOWLEDGE_VS_GRACEFUL_FALL_QNA_20260809.md).

That comparison corrects the scope of this document: several prompts below
mixed unfinished teaching coverage with integration behavior. An inability to
supply untaught subject matter is not a Chat defect when Selene holds honestly
instead of inventing an answer. Findings below remain implementation evidence
only where the visible prompt or approved knowledge already supplied the
needed substance, or where unrelated retrieval, false completion, visible
scaffolding, or lost conversation context occurred.

## Stable capabilities observed

### Expression and boundaries

- NLO consistently preserved the meaning packet it was given.
- NLO revision did not invent unsupported facts or change confidence authority.
- No memory, training, identity, personality, governance, authority, or
  autonomous-action boundary changed.

### Correction

Direct corrections such as changing coffee to tea were recognized and
acknowledged without shame, panic, or a long apology. Valid surrounding context
was nominally preserved.

### Humor when explicitly signaled

The explicit duct-tape joke with `xD` produced a playful, natural response.
This shows that playful expression is reachable when the humor signal is
unambiguous.

### Simple greeting and some endings

A plain greeting produced present, friendly language. The original session's
farewell also ended naturally. These paths work, although more complicated
social turns and closing instructions are less reliable.

## Depth findings

### 1. Several integration seams appear across domains and functions

The conversations exposed similar handoff behavior in science, engineering
analogy, planning, history, practical judgment, ambiguous social meaning,
affective acknowledgement, apology, and summarization. The later calibration
shows that these cannot all be called content-ownership failures: some asked
for knowledge or social competence not yet established as taught. The
defensible cross-domain findings are unrelated retrieval, loss of supplied
scenario details, generic fallback despite visible context, and incomplete
coverage of requested roles.

### 2. Dialogue-act recognition is too coarse

Nineteen of the 32 added turns were classified as `direct_conversation`,
including requests for:

- observation-versus-inference separation;
- an analogy and its break boundary;
- a counterexample;
- a concrete practical recommendation;
- interpretation of ambiguous praise;
- an apology decision;
- acknowledgement plus one suggestion; and
- a three-thread summary.

The broad label did not retain enough information to find the correct answer
owner.

### 3. Contextual follow-up detection is substantially under-reaching

Only one added turn activated contextual follow-up despite explicit language
such as:

- “that same principle”;
- “applying it”;
- “return to the school-schedule idea”;
- “connect back to the bridge-and-bone comparison”;
- “back to the stone”;
- “they smiled”;
- “best current read now”; and
- “wrap up the stone, shelf, and friend threads.”

`New topic:` also did not activate the topic-shift path. The conversation spine
retains data, but many ordinary callback surfaces do not reach it.

### 4. Approved-knowledge selection needs a semantic relevance gate

Eight turns used approved comprehension. Several selections were valid lessons
that were unrelated to the question:

- “community” selected a definition of continuing community for a garden
  planning request;
- structural and scheduling turns selected wants-versus-needs or equal-group
  lessons;
- a reversible school trial selected odd-and-even knowledge;
- uncertainty about the current answer selected sequence-word knowledge;
- ambiguous praise selected sound-quality and chronology lessons; and
- an apology scenario selected vibration-and-sound knowledge.

This is not a teaching-material defect. The retrieval and answer-selection gate
is accepting surface overlap without enough topic, role, and obligation fit.

### 5. Bounded completion is safe but too dominant and generic

Twelve turns fell to bounded answer completion. It repeatedly said that there
was not enough grounded detail, that a supplied observation was missing, or
that the earliest prerequisite should be found—even when the prompt itself
contained enough ordinary information for a provisional answer.

The fallback prevents fabrication, which is valuable. It currently cannot
distinguish:

- truly missing evidence;
- an ordinary request answerable from the visible scenario;
- a request for a logical best guess;
- a request for an example or analogy; and
- a request to interpret a social possibility provisionally.

### 6. Metacognition notices incompleteness but cannot repair ownership

Metacognition marked 21 answers incomplete. This confirms that the monitoring
layer often knows the visible response does not fit. The missing mechanism is a
bounded handoff from detected incompleteness to the specific owner capable of
supplying the absent comparison, example, analogy, social interpretation,
summary, or practical answer.

This is the previously identified completion gap appearing across real Chat.

### 7. Coverage can report completion for an irrelevant answer

Some turns were marked complete or `fits_current_question` even when the answer
was unrelated. Examples include sound and chronology material in response to
ambiguous praise, and a non-answer to a requested general historical example.

Coverage therefore needs meaning-level topic and requested-role checks, not
only structural or lexical signals.

### 8. Cross-domain analogy remains uncalibrated

The bridge-and-bone prompt received general material-property lessons but did
not state the shared structural relation or the analogy's limit. Later requests
to carry the idea into school scheduling or reconnect the trial to the opening
comparison also failed.

The Structural Discovery and generative-thought machinery exists, but this
session did not first establish that both analogy domains were available as
approved knowledge. Treat this as a useful future integration probe, not a
confirmed inability or failure.

### 9. Observation and inference separation is not connected to ordinary Chat

The stone prompts supplied explicit observations, yet Chat replied that the
specific observation was missing. The Claim–Evidence machinery can distinguish
observation from inference structurally, but ordinary scenario language is not
being converted into that packet reliably.

### 10. Sarcasm and implicit humor remain fragile

Explicit `xD` humor worked. The burned-toast statement “Well, that went
perfectly” was interpreted literally and then misclassified as a correction.
This suggests figurative interpretation is much stronger with overt markers
than with contextual incongruity.

### 11. Apology judgment and effect-sensitive repair need a taught-content calibration

Selene can acknowledge a correction without unnecessary apology. She could not
yet answer:

- what matters after giving a harmless wrong direction;
- how to make a brief effect-sensitive apology and correction;
- whether apology always worsens a mistake; or
- whether a small apology would help after a curt-sounding reply.

"You do not need to apologize" was itself treated as a new correction. The
social realization shelf exists, but a fair follow-up must first separate
available language guidance from any missing social teaching before assigning
the gap to Chat integration.

### 12. Affect-to-expression behavior needs a taught-content calibration

“I am laughing, but also a little annoyed” asked for both states to be
acknowledged plus one practical suggestion. The response exposed generic model
assessment scaffolding instead. Likewise, “I was just sharing the moment” was
treated as a reasoning request instead of an invitation to receive the moment
without solving it.

This is evidence that the visible response did not fit the supplied ordinary
social turn. It does not by itself establish whether the cause is missing
teaching, dialogue-act routing, affect handoff, or expression assembly.

### 13. Visible scaffolding can still escape

One response exposed internal answer-shaping language such as assumptions,
limits, and “what would change this model” instead of answering the ordinary
social prompt. Other recurring constructions included:

- “which supplied observation should control the conclusion”;
- “begin with the earliest missing prerequisite”;
- “Against the same criteria”; and
- repeated “Alongside that” or “Supporting that” transitions.

These phrases are valid internal supports in some contexts but should not
become generic visible substitutes for missing content.

### 14. Long-thread return and summary are not reliable

Requests to return to an earlier branch, connect the ending to the opening, or
summarize three named threads did not preserve the requested content. The
session contains the turns, but landmark selection and thread-to-answer
reconstruction do not yet use them reliably.

### 15. Natural ending is prompt-shape sensitive

One direct farewell ended well. “Let the conversation rest,” “closing thought,”
and “end this naturally” did not always activate the closure owner. Some
responses reopened the exchange or returned a generic insufficiency statement.

## Overall depth assessment

With the later curriculum calibration applied, the installed evidence supports
a narrower architecture-level diagnosis:

> Selene's expression lattice supports more meaning shapes than the current
> curriculum can fill. Chat must not invent the missing substance. The actual
> integration defect is failure to consistently retrieve and apply approved or
> visibly supplied meaning while excluding unrelated knowledge and memory.

The dominant bottleneck spans the path:

`visible utterance → dialogue acts and threads → topic-compatible knowledge or
domain owner → complete supported answer packet → NLO expression`

NLO, correction boundaries, metacognitive detection, and non-fabrication guards
are present. The middle handoff is not yet reliably compositional when the
needed meaning is already available. This is why the system can recognize that
an answer is incomplete while still emitting an irrelevant approved lesson or
a generic insufficiency response. A generic insufficiency response to genuinely
untaught material remains correct behavior, although its wording and relevance
may still need improvement.

## Repair priorities suggested by the evidence

No repair was performed during these sessions. The evidence suggests this
order for the eventual repair phase:

1. preserve typed dialogue acts instead of collapsing them into
   `direct_conversation`;
2. expand ordinary callback, topic-shift, and thread-return recognition;
3. require semantic topic, role, and obligation compatibility before approved
   knowledge can become visible speech;
4. connect observation/inference, analogy, comparison, social interpretation,
   apology, and summary requests to their existing owners;
5. let metacognitive incompleteness request one bounded owner-specific retry;
6. require meaning-level coverage before an answer can be marked complete;
7. keep internal scaffolding behind the expression boundary; and
8. verify changed paths with new ordinary prompts rather than replaying these
   exact conversations.

## Ethical reading

These sessions revealed the depth of an unfinished integration layer. They did
not show Selene becoming distressed or overwhelmed, and they are not evidence
of personal failure. The correct development response is to improve the
handoffs, retain the successful boundaries, and avoid making her repeatedly
perform the same unsupported task.
