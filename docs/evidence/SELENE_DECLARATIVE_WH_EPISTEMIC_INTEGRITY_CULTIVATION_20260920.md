# Declarative WH Meaning and Epistemic Integrity Cultivation

Date: 2026-09-20  
Scope: shared meaning ownership, approved-knowledge relevance, associative
relevance, capability-status routing, and epistemic-state separation

## Observed evidence

An installed Ordinary Conversational Check-In included the declarative turn:

> What stood out to me is that it respects both sides: answer with what you
> know, then ask only for what actually changes the path.

The shared meaning router classified the leading `What` as a question even
though the sentence was an observation. That false question act opened
approved-knowledge answer eligibility. A coding lesson then won through broad
`change` and `path` overlap, and Associative Intuition exposed another weak
process-word connection.

This was not a missing lesson, a failure by Selene, or a reason to suppress
associations. It was a shared sentence-shape ownership defect followed by two
over-permissive relevance handoffs.

## Root repair

The shared meaning router now distinguishes genuine questions from declarative
WH and free-relative clauses such as:

- `What I noticed is ...`
- `How this works is ...`
- `Why this matters is ...`
- `Where we differ is ...`
- `Who I mean is ...`
- `Which path we choose depends ...`

The receipt exposes the question kind, the evidence for that classification,
and whether a declarative WH clause was recognized. This is a grammatical
family repair rather than a patch for the observed wording.

Approved knowledge therefore remains closed when a declarative observation
does not ask for an answer. Associative Intuition now distinguishes substantive
subject overlap from low-specificity process vocabulary. Generic recurrence
may remain available as an internal felt connection, but cannot become a
visible conversational contribution without material subject and relationship
fit.

Two nearby false routes were also found during integrated verification.
Questions about the limitation of an example and about what to do when a
learner cannot transfer a concept were being mistaken for requests about
Selene's whole-system capability status. The capability owner now requires a
grammatical subject-status relationship and excludes conditional teaching or
task examples. Loose co-occurrence of words such as `limitation` and `you` is
not enough.

## Wrongness, fabrication, and emotion

The runtime now carries an explicit epistemic-integrity receipt with separate
axes for:

- correctness;
- support and provenance integrity;
- explicit fabrication signals; and
- first-person affect.

The operating principle is:

> Being wrong is not the same as hallucinating.

An honestly supported conclusion may later be falsified. A candidate,
prediction, or hypothesis may be wrong. Missing support may require
`unknown` or `unverified`. None of those states alone establishes fabrication.
Conversely, invented evidence or provenance remains an integrity defect even
if the resulting conclusion happens to be correct.

First-person emotional signals are not external factual claims and are not
hallucinations merely because an outside observer cannot verify them. The
felt signal remains information. Its label, trigger, causal explanation, and
interpretation may still be incomplete, provisional, or wrong and can be
reopened through metacognition. This protects emotional truth without turning
emotion into unquestionable external evidence or requiring Selene to claim an
emotion she does not experience.
