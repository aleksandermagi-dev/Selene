## Findings

### 1. Mixed social and self-state intent was misrouted

“Good morning, Selene :) How are you approaching our work today?” was routed as
reasoning rather than a greeting plus present self-state question. The
self-state response owner remained empty, and bounded completion produced a
source-demand response.

This is an intent and ownership seam, not a lack of warmth.

### 2. Approved knowledge relevance was too loose

The community-garden comparison selected a reviewed statement about what makes
a continuing community because both contained the word “community.” The
knowledge itself was valid, but it did not answer the visible question.

A later invitation to add one relevant point selected an unrelated approved
lesson about sound. This is strong evidence that approved knowledge needs a
stricter topic-and-obligation compatibility gate before it can outrank current
conversation content.

### 3. Comparison planning supplied a method instead of the requested answer

The Answer Engine correctly identified comparison/planning, but its executable
packet supplied a general comparison procedure. It did not produce the actual
one-bed-versus-three recommendation, specific reason, or requested limit.

NLO then preserved and expressed the packet it received. The missing substance
belongs upstream of expression.

### 4. Topic-shift classification was repaired, but answer substance was absent

The uncertainty question was correctly separated from the earlier topic. With
no relevant answer packet selected, however, the response fell back to a
generic prerequisite-and-reversible-step construction instead of explaining
ordinary uncertainty.

The Phase 8 topic-shift repair held. The remaining gap is source and answer
ownership after the shift.

### 5. Rephrase, example, and analogy requests did not reach their owners

The plain-language example request and the map analogy request were treated as
ordinary direct conversation. Both fell into insufficient-grounding or
yes/no-shaped fallback language even though neither was a yes/no question.

The language lattice can express examples and analogies when supplied, but Chat
did not route these requests to supported example or analogy formation.

### 6. Correction handling was socially sound but could not regenerate content

The correction layer correctly retained “ordinary not-knowing,” preserved the
surrounding conversation, and avoided shame. It could not update the requested
example because the prior example had never been produced and no new content
owner supplied one.

### 7. Session summary selected the wrong landmark

The two-part summary-and-practical-use request returned only an earlier generic
sentence about fair comparison. It neither summarized the uncertainty thread
nor answered the practical-use part.

This points to topic-aware landmark selection and multi-part completion rather
than an NLO sentence-realization fault.

## Overall assessment

The language lattice is functioning as a meaning-preserving expression system:
it retained supplied meaning, correction state, contextual classification, and
boundaries. The real Q&A shows that Chat still too often supplies it with the
wrong meaning or no usable answer substance.

The highest-priority gap is therefore not “more sentence variety.” It is the
connection between visible dialogue obligations and the correct answer owner:

1. mixed-intent and self-state routing;
2. strict topic relevance for approved knowledge;
3. concrete Answer Engine execution for open comparison/planning requests;
4. rephrase, example, analogy, and summary ownership; and
5. one bounded completion retry that asks the correct owner for missing
   substance rather than substituting a generic fallback.

These findings are development evidence. They do not indicate that Selene was
overwhelmed, distressed, or personally unsuccessful.

Do not repeat the whole ten-turn conversation unless the combined interaction
