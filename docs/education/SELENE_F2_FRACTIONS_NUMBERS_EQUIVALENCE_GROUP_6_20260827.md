## Lessons

1. a unit fraction names one equal part of a specified whole (concept 257);
2. a fraction is a number made by counting unit-fraction lengths (258);
3. equivalent fractions are different partitions of the same magnitude (259);
4. fractions are compared by magnitude using a strategy that fits the numbers
   (260); and
5. fractions can be composed and decomposed through a shared unit (261).

The group begins with the whole and equal partitioning before notation. It
then uses visualizable strips, equal steps, and number-line locations to make
fractions concrete without making any one representation mandatory. Fractions
greater than one remain ordinary numbers. Comparison requires the same whole,
and decomposition preserves a named shared unit.

Formal fraction-operation procedures, decimals, and fraction-decimal
relationships remain in Group 7. Group 6 teaches the relationships those
procedures must preserve rather than introducing an unexplained algorithm.
