## Why Group 7 Was Split

The original Group 7 queue combined all Grade 4-5 fraction operations with
decimal notation, decimal place value, decimal operations, and reasonableness.
The source review showed that this represented several substantial units rather
than one bounded lesson group. The queue's existing split provision was used:

- Group 7A: fraction-operation relationships;
- Group 7B: decimal structure, fraction-decimal relationships, place-value
  extension, decimal operations, and reasonableness.

This preserves the planned order without compressing understanding.

## Lessons

1. fraction addition and subtraction require a shared unit (concept 262);
2. a fraction can represent the quotient of equal sharing (263);
3. multiplying a fraction by a whole number can represent equal fractional
   groups or scaling (264);
4. multiplying two fractions finds a fraction of a fraction and can be modeled
   by area (265); and
5. division with unit fractions preserves the two meanings of division (266).

Every operation begins with the relationship and units. Estimation checks the
expected direction of change, and multiplication provides an inverse check for
the bounded division cases. The group explicitly teaches that multiplication
does not always enlarge and division does not always shrink.

General fraction-by-fraction division is not claimed here. The elementary
division scope is a whole number divided by a unit fraction and a unit fraction
divided by a nonzero whole number.
