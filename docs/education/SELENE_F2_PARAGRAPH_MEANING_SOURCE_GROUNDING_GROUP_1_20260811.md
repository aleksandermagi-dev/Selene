## Why This Group Comes First

F2 begins with reading and source-grounding because later mathematics,
science, history, research, and multi-step conversation all depend on finding
what a paragraph means, preserving its limits, and asking precisely for missing
information. This group builds on F1 sentence purpose, text purpose,
reconstruction, question roles, observation-versus-interpretation, and
source-versus-inference boundaries.

## Five Reviewable Lessons

1. a paragraph develops a main idea through related supporting details;
2. explicit information and bounded inference have different evidence
   relationships;
3. a summary preserves load-bearing meaning without copying every detail;
4. quotation and paraphrase preserve different parts of a source relationship,
   and both retain attribution; and
5. a focused question asks for the smallest missing detail that materially
   changes the answer.

The definitions live in `src/selene/curriculum_f2_group1.py` and use the
existing Acquire -> Integrate -> Express -> comprehension lifecycle.
