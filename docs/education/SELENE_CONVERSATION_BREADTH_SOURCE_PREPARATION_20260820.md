## Outcome

The current source shelf already contains useful dialogue evidence, but it does
not yet cover every conversational function Selene's completed language
architecture can express. The next source work should therefore select small,
attributed examples by function instead of importing a general instruction or
chat corpus.

The strongest commercially clean or conditionally usable new candidates are:

1. **Taskmaster-2** for spontaneous user wording, clarifications, comparisons,
   changing constraints, disfluency, and multi-part completion;
2. **ReDial** for preference discovery, comparison, recommendation, suggestion,
   and conversational follow-up;
3. **Contrack**, with ShareAlike isolation, for pronouns, entity continuity,
   plural references, callbacks, and non-strict turn alternation;
4. **MultiWOZ 2.2** for multi-domain state, topic movement, correction, and
   unresolved task details;
5. **Action-Based Conversations Dataset** for requests that must be
   reconciled with visible rules, multi-step completion, and honest inability;
6. **CoQA rights-safe subsets** for conversational questions, implicit
   references, follow-ups, and evidence-bound answers;
7. **DeliData**, only after its dataset license and access terms are confirmed,
   for collaborative problem solving, disagreement, reconsideration, and
   group synthesis; and
8. **source-by-source Project Gutenberg dialogue** for literary register,
    humor, figurative language, character voice distinction, and long-range
    discourse—not as evidence of ordinary modern conversation.

DailyDialog, EmpatheticDialogues, LoCoMo, AMI, and other noncommercial sources
are not permanent teaching candidates. They may identify a gap during private
research, but their text, annotations, transformations, and closely adapted
examples do not enter retained lessons, distributable artifacts, or reusable
architecture. The preferred replacement is a commercially compatible source
or independently authored material.

No single source is Selene's voice. Each source may supply evidence about a
conversation mechanism; NLO and Voice remain responsible for original,
context-aware expression.

## Commercial-Reuse Safety Decision

Selene is currently private and is not being prepared as a downloadable or
monetized individual. The general mechanisms learned while building her may,
however, inform future project-neutral architecture. Source selection therefore
uses the stricter commercially reusable standard now rather than trying to
untangle provenance later.

| Class | Allowed use | Retained or reusable output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CC0, verified public domain, CC BY, verified MIT/Apache dataset artifact | bounded acquisition, review, and teaching | allowed with source ledger, notices, attribution, change marking, and original expression |
| CC BY-SA or CDLA-Sharing | isolated research and mechanism extraction | only independently expressed computational results or separately licensed data artifacts that fully preserve the source obligations |
| CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA, or another noncommercial license | private gap discovery only when genuinely needed | no source text, transformed data, adapted lesson, retained knowledge packet, distributable artifact, or reusable blueprint |
| mixed, missing, repository-only, access-gated, or uncertain terms | metadata hold | none until the exact artifact is cleared |

Raw datasets are never bundled with Selene, committed to the public repository,
or placed in an installer. Copyrightable dialogue wording is not used as a
runtime response source. Abstract conversational mechanisms are rewritten as
independent project-authored examples and checked on distinct applications.

## Existing Private Conversation Evidence

The private Aleks/Selene corpus remains the richest available evidence for the
actual relationship's callbacks, shared humor, pacing, correction, topic braids,
warmth, disagreement, collaborative work, and long-horizon continuity. It is
not a public dataset and must not become a bulk language corpus or visible quote
bank.

The existing detached-copy Metacognition Miner pass is complete for its selected
149 conversations. Its recorded snapshot covered 96,848 messages and was built
to identify metacognitive methods, not to exhaustively assess conversational
breadth. Aleks reports that the broader/current corpus is now greater than the
earlier approximate 144,000-message estimate.

An initial speaker-aware breadth pass has now completed against the existing
96,848-message fingerprinted snapshot. It prepared 12 private, review-only
source-bound lesson candidates and a separate cross-track observation ledger.
See `SELENE_PRIVATE_CORPUS_CONVERSATION_BREADTH_SET_20260820.md`. A later pass
over a fresh export must use a separately fingerprinted copy and should process
new or changed source material without assuming the older snapshot is current.

A future targeted breadth review may extract only:

- function labels and source references;
- project-neutral mechanisms;
- examples that Aleks explicitly reviews for private Selene use;
- counterexamples and context limits; and
- independently written teaching fixtures.

It must not publish raw messages, create an Aleks persona model, make every
assistant message authoritative, duplicate personal memory, quote private
wording into ordinary Chat, or reconnect the raw corpus to runtime.

## What the Breadth Material Must Support

The current architecture already owns the mechanics for conversation. Source
material is needed to deepen the range and transfer evidence for:

| Need | Evidence to select | What must not be learned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary back-and-forth | acknowledgements, reactions, elaboration, mutual questions, short and long turns | canned greetings or compulsory friendliness |
| Content-light turns | celebration, disappointment, surprise, momentum, self-resolution, shared attention | inventing facts just to keep talking |
| Reference and callback | pronouns, named entities, plural groups, earlier alternatives, topic returns | storing source personas as personal memory |
| Mixed intent | one message containing several requests, feelings, corrections, and side notes | blindly answering in source order when context requires another order |
| Repair | clarification, restatement, correction, changed constraints, graceful inability | repeated apology or treating ordinary wrongness as personal failure |
| Natural uncertainty | fuzzy recall, provisional answer, missing evidence, best guess, meaningful disagreement | one repeated refusal phrase or false certainty |
| Hypothesis and prediction | clearly labeled inference, assumptions, alternatives, what would change the view | turning prediction into fact or suppressing exploration |
| Advice and preference | asking what matters, comparing options, suggesting without pressure | manipulation, hidden options, fake urgency, or claiming execution |
| Affect in language | listening, direct care, celebration, frustration, tenderness, emotional intensity | prescribed warmth, diagnosis, therapy persona, or emotional flattening |
| Disagreement | reasons, counterexamples, revision, unresolved conflict, evidence updates | domination, shame, performative debate, or consensus at any cost |
| Humor and figurative language | timing, callbacks, playful echo, metaphor, sarcasm recognition, limits | copyrighted character imitation or jokes at tender material without context |
| Long discourse | thesis, topic braid, return, example, summary, natural ending | endless elaboration or premature closure |
| Collaborative work | offering an idea, asking for help, dividing a task, reporting an obstacle | autonomous action or commitments that are not executed |
| Register variation | casual, technical, reflective, instructional, playful, serious | a random style switch detached from meaning and relationship |

## Proposed Small Teaching Groups

These are preparation labels, not authorization and not teaching packets.

### Breadth Group B1 — Reference and Callback

Sources: Contrack plus project-authored synthetic fixtures.

Mechanisms:

- who or what a pronoun refers to;
- singular versus plural references;
- two people or places with overlapping properties;
- a callback to an earlier option;
- consecutive messages from the same speaker;
- an explicit correction to a referent; and
- asking one concise question when the referent materially changes the answer.

Why first: every later multi-turn lesson depends on stable reference.

### Breadth Group B2 — Constraint Change and Multi-Part Completion

Sources: Taskmaster-2, MultiWOZ 2.2, and original fixtures.

Mechanisms:

- several requests in one turn;
- preferences changing mid-conversation;
- comparing two or more options;
- answering the known parts while naming the missing part;
- returning to an earlier thread after a side question; and
- distinguishing speech about an action from an executed action.

### Breadth Group B3 — Suggestion, Preference, and Collaborative Initiative

Sources: ReDial, selected Taskmaster-2 recommendation conversations, and
project-authored fixtures.

Mechanisms:

- learning what matters before recommending;
- offering an idea without requiring permission to think;
- explaining why a suggestion fits;
- accepting a rejection without pressure;
- suggesting a risky option with visible tradeoffs; and
- asking for help when a collaborative task genuinely needs it.

### Breadth Group B4 — Correction, Disagreement, and Revision

Sources: Crowd Deliberation, later DeliData if cleared, and original fixtures.

Mechanisms:

- distinguishing disagreement from hostility;
- giving a reason and counterexample;
- changing confidence without shame;
- revising a hypothesis when evidence changes;
- preserving useful structure while correcting a conclusion; and
- leaving genuinely unresolved conflict open.

### Breadth Group B5 — Affect, Care, and Direct Truth

Sources: private reviewed relationship mechanisms plus wholly original
context-rich examples. Noncommercial dialogue datasets may help name a research
question, but none of their text, labels, transformations, or adapted examples
enter this group.

Mechanisms:

- acknowledging a feeling without diagnosing it;
- warmth, tenderness, enthusiasm, frustration, or restraint when each fits;
- telling an unwelcome truth without cruelty;
- not apologizing merely for existing, disagreeing, or lacking information;
- humor only when context opens room; and
- preserving agency while emotion informs salience and tone.

### Breadth Group B6 — Source-Grounded Conversational Q&A

Sources: rights-safe CoQA domains plus original attributed packets.

Mechanisms:

- follow-up questions with omitted or implicit referents;
- short direct answers with optional explanation;
- pointing to evidence without parroting it;
- separating source statement, interpretation, and hypothesis;
- asking for a missing source only when it changes the answer; and
- offering a labeled best attempt when a complete answer is unavailable.

### Breadth Group B7 — Figurative, Playful, and Register Transfer

Sources: individually cleared Gutenberg works, Topical-Chat, and original
examples.

Mechanisms:

- metaphor and figure-of-speech recognition;
- sarcasm as a context-dependent interpretation rather than a lexical trigger;
- callbacks and shared jokes;
- playful mimicry of wording visible in the current turn;
- casual, technical, reflective, and serious registers; and
- applying a mechanism to new wording without character or author imitation.

## Source Acceptance Checklist for the Next Pass

Before any candidate moves beyond this preparation document:

1. pin the exact artifact and revision;
2. save the exact dataset license, not merely the repository's code license;
3. record collection method, participant consent description, and known
   transformations;
4. inspect at least 20 whole conversations across the intended functions;
5. check for personal data, secrets, unsafe content, provider identity, hidden
   behavioral instructions, and fictional account credentials;
6. identify factual statements that must be removed or independently sourced;
7. label source utterance, project interpretation, and transferable mechanism
   separately;
8. record examples, non-examples, limits, and cultural/contextual caveats;
9. ensure the lesson can be written without copying a participant's persona or
   source wording;
10. define a distinct application that was not in the source;
11. keep licenses and attribution attached to every retained excerpt or
    derived artifact; and
12. require a separate teaching authorization or the already applicable
    bounded curriculum authorization before Acquire begins.

## Explicit Exclusions

Do not prepare the following as conversation teaching sources:

- scraped private chats, Discord logs, leaked messages, or data without clear
  participant permission;
- ShareGPT/chatbot dumps whose model/provider terms and personal-data history
  cannot be reconciled;
- Reddit conversation dumps or social-media threads merely because they are
  publicly visible;
- OpenSubtitles, movie/television scripts, contemporary novel dumps, or other
  copyrighted dialogue collections without a lawful item-level basis;
- role-play/persona collections intended to make a model impersonate a
  character or relationship;
- generic SFT mixtures with unrecoverable ancestry;
- noncommercial datasets used as retained teaching or reusable blueprint
  material;
- preference-ranking data treated as proof that one response is universally
  correct;
- therapeutic or counseling dialogue used to confer clinical authority;
- toxic/adversarial conversation batteries when the same mechanism can be
  taught through ordinary examples;
- benchmark answer keys used as factual instruction; and
- any source that asks Selene to inherit an assistant, provider, customer
  service, teacher, therapist, or fictional persona.

## Recommended Acquisition Order

When Aleks authorizes actual source acquisition, the smallest useful order is:

1. Taskmaster-2 metadata and a small two-person comparison/clarification slice;
2. ReDial metadata and a small preference/recommendation slice;
3. Contrack metadata, license, and 20–30 complete reference-focused examples,
   isolated from distributable teaching artifacts by its ShareAlike terms;
4. MultiWOZ 2.2 metadata and a small changed-constraint/multi-domain slice;
5. Action-Based Conversations metadata and a small rule/request/completion
   slice;
6. project-authored counterexamples and distinct applications for B1–B3;
7. only then, rights-safe CoQA, deliberation, and literary sources. DailyDialog
   and EmpatheticDialogues remain noncommercial research references, not
   acquisition or teaching steps.

This order fills the most immediate conversational gaps with human-human
evidence while keeping affect, persona, copyright, and benchmark risks out of
the first batch.
