## Ordered Place

This group follows Group 10's pushes, pulls, force interactions, and motion
effects. Repeated back-and-forth motion provides a concrete bridge into sound
and vibration. Material properties and position models provide the bridge into
illumination, blocked light, shadows, and communication design.

The six lessons are:

1. vibration and sound as a two-way observable cause-and-effect relationship;
2. safe comparisons of loudness, duration, and higher or lower tone;
3. light sources, illumination, visibility, and darkness;
4. transparent, translucent, opaque, and reflective material behavior;
5. shadows as regions receiving less light because a path is blocked; and
6. light and sound patterns used as signals in tested communication designs.

## Source-Aligned Boundaries

The source explicitly treats Grade 1 work as experiential rather than a full
theoretical account. It supports vibration and sound, visibility under
illumination, material effects on a light beam, shadows, and using light or
sound to communicate across distance.

The lessons deliberately preserve these limits:

- observation is separated from later wave explanations;
- loudness, duration, and higher or lower tone are not collapsed together;
- louder does not mean higher, better, more important, or more correct;
- light sources are distinguished from illuminated or reflective objects;
- transparent, translucent, opaque, and reflective describe different
  behaviors and remain tied to visible light and test conditions;
- a shadow is a region receiving less light, not an object or substance;
- signal detection is distinguished from interpretation and successful
  communication;
- bright-light and loud-sound investigations retain explicit sensory-safety
  limits; and
- frequency, wavelength, energy, electromagnetic theory, quantitative optics
  and acoustics, hearing diagnosis, lasers, and safety-critical signals remain
  later or specialist topics.
