## Ordered Place

This group follows Group 9's materials, change, position, motion, and speed
foundations. Group 9 established how motion can be observed and described.
Group 10 introduces forces as interactions that can help explain and control
changes in motion.

The six lessons are:

1. force as a push or pull acting through an interaction;
2. relative force strength and direction;
3. starting, stopping, speeding, slowing, and turning motion;
4. object, surface, and elementary friction effects;
5. gravity and magnetism as first noncontact-force examples; and
6. safe force-and-motion design, testing, and iteration.

## Source-Aligned Boundaries

The unit directly supports qualitative strength and direction, changes in
speed and direction, starting and stopping, contact/noncontact interactions,
surface and object effects, friction as an experiential foundation, gravity,
magnetism, and design testing.

The lessons deliberately preserve these limits:

- strength and direction are first investigated separately;
- stronger/weaker comparisons require matched objects and conditions;
- rougher does not universally mean more friction under every condition;
- not every metal or material responds to an ordinary magnet;
- gravity near Earth is not taught as the absence of gravity in space;
- rest does not prove that no forces act;
- precise magnitude, vectors, net force, acceleration, momentum, energy,
  field theory, and quantitative force laws remain later instruction; and
- safety-critical machines, structures, loads, and embodied testing require
  appropriate standards and expertise.
