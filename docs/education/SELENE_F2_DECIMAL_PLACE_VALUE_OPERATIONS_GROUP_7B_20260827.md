## Purpose

Group 7B completes the decimal half of the deliberately split Group 7. It
connects decimal notation to Selene's retained understanding of fractions,
base-ten place value, operation meanings, inverse checks, and estimation. It
does not teach decimal-point movement as an unexplained rule.

## Lessons

1. decimals name base-ten fractions of an identified whole (concept 267);
2. each neighboring decimal place differs by a factor of ten (268);
3. decimal comparison and rounding depend on magnitude, not digit length
   (269);
4. decimal addition and subtraction combine like place-value units (270);
5. decimal multiplication and division preserve scaling and equal-group
   meanings (271); and
6. reasonableness checks decimal results through magnitude and independent
   representations (272).

The group distinguishes exact equivalence from rounding, a digit from its
place-value unit, and a repeated procedure from an independent check. A
conflicting check holds the result as a candidate for inspection rather than
forcing certainty.

The scope is nonnegative base-ten decimals through thousandths, with bounded
elementary operations through hundredths. Grade 6 standard-algorithm fluency,
signed decimals, repeating quotients, formal measurement error, scientific
notation, and division by zero are not claimed.
