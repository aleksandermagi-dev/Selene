Date: 2026-08-08

## Ordered Place

This group follows observation, classification, evidence, materials, change,
motion, forces, light, sound, simple machines, and bounded design. It begins
life science by relating living systems to their needs and environments without
treating a single visible feature as a complete biological test.

The six lessons are:

1. living, nonliving, formerly living, dormant, and uncertain classification;
2. differing needs and resources of plants, animals, and humans;
3. habitat-resource fit across conditions and time;
4. organism parts, functions, information, and responses;
5. growth, development, young-adult similarities, and individual variation;
   and
6. care and habitat design grounded in evidence, restraint, and qualified help.

## Source-Aligned Boundaries

The sources support organism needs, habitats, external parts and functions,
environmental responses, young-adult similarities and differences, care
behavior, observation, models, and bounded design. The lesson set adds
important scientific and ethical distinctions:

- movement or one other visible feature is not a complete life test;
- dormant, dead, formerly living, nonliving, and uncertain remain distinct;
- needs, preferences, resources, and ways of obtaining resources remain
  distinct;
- habitat fit belongs to a named organism, life stage, conditions, and time;
- a structure can support a function without guaranteeing an outcome;
- plants respond to conditions without requiring human-like senses or intent;
- young organisms resemble their kind without being exact copies;
- concern for an organism does not by itself establish the correct
  intervention; and
- uncertainty and later correction remain legitimate learning states.

Genetics, DNA, inheritance mechanisms, detailed reproduction, metamorphosis,
hybrids, taxonomy, species-level care, internal anatomy, diagnosis, nutrition
planning, wildlife handling, harmful deprivation, ecological policy, and real
habitat intervention remain later or specialist instruction.
