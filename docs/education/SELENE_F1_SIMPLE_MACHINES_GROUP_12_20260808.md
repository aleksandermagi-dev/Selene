## Ordered Place

This group follows materials, motion, forces, light, sound, and tested design.
It turns those foundations into inspectable mechanical systems while preserving
the tradeoffs hidden by the ordinary phrase "makes work easier."

The six lessons are:

1. machines as task-oriented input/output arrangements with force tradeoffs;
2. ramps and inclined planes;
3. wheels, axles, gears, and connected rotation;
4. levers, fulcrums, input locations, and loads;
5. pulleys, wedges, screws, and redirected force; and
6. compound machines, components, constraints, testing, and revision.

## Source-Aligned Boundaries

The source supports ramps, wheels and axles, pulleys, levers, wedges, screws,
gears, compound machines, task fit, comparison, and elementary engineering
design. The lessons add scientifically important guardrails without importing
later calculation procedures:

- a machine may reduce required force, change direction, increase control, or
  improve access while increasing distance or introducing another tradeoff;
- "easier" does not mean energy or scientific work disappears;
- a fixed pulley primarily changes direction and does not automatically reduce
  ideal input-force magnitude;
- a wheel, axle, gear, lever, fulcrum, wedge, screw, pulley, component, and
  compound machine retain distinct meanings;
- shapes that resemble mechanisms are not automatically performing those
  functions in the actual arrangement;
- unexpected results reopen the component and connection model; and
- powered equipment, people-lifting, rigging, overhead loads, blades,
  structural ratings, electrical work, and safety-critical construction remain
  outside this teaching group.

Mechanical advantage, work, energy, power, efficiency, torque, stress,
friction coefficients, gear ratios, line tension, thread pitch, ergonomics,
and failure calculations remain later instruction.
