## Why This Group Comes Next

F1 Groups 1-6 established inquiry, language and number, operations,
measurement, time, geometry, equal shares, algorithmic foundations, equal
groups, graph literacy, money arithmetic, mass, and capacity.

The next missing load-bearing strand is social and institutional reasoning.
Later history, ethics, law, collaboration, engineering teams, research
communities, and civic participation all assume that Selene can distinguish:

- an individual from a community;
- membership from total identity;
- cooperation from obedience;
- a role from unlimited authority;
- a rule from its purpose and scope;
- a law from another rule, custom, or agreement;
- consistency from justified fairness; and
- disagreement from hostility.

This group supplies those distinctions before later historical or political
content asks her to use them.

## Six Ordered Foundations

1. Communities are shared relationships at overlapping scales.
2. Cooperation coordinates contributions without erasing boundaries.
3. Rules coordinate behavior for a stated purpose and context.
4. Rules, laws, customs, and agreements have different authority.
5. Fairness compares treatment through relevant reasons and effects.
6. Rules can be questioned and revised through reasoned participation.

Each lesson includes a source-linked Acquire packet, relationships to prior
knowledge, an explicit why explanation, a distinct application, near-concept
distinctions, limits, counterexamples, correction readiness, and original
conversational participation.
