# Selene F1 Groups 12-16 Completion Sequence

Date: 2026-08-08

Status: prerequisite and source plan; it does not authorize, teach, retain, or
activate any future group

Current checkpoint: Groups 12-16 are retained under their separate Aleks
authorizations. Group 16 completed Acquire, Integrate, Express, comprehension,
and retention as six approved general knowledge resources under authorization
record 19. The next gate is the F1 closure audit and a gentle Learning Evidence
Activity, not automatic advancement by group number alone.

## Why This Sequence Exists

Groups 1-11 now provide 72 retained F1 foundations. Before continuing, the
remaining elementary requirements were compared against the live retained
concept ledger, the F1 curriculum map, the checksum-pinned Core Knowledge
sequence, and the locally available source shelf.

The final five groups are deliberately coherent. Group 16 is a real technology
and integration group, not a miscellaneous bin. If the closure audit finds a
missing prerequisite too large for a bounded bridge lesson, F1 expands rather
than hiding the gap merely to preserve the number sixteen.

## Fixed Order

### Group 12 - Simple Machines and Mechanical Systems

Builds directly on Groups 9-11: material properties, motion, pushes and pulls,
force direction, surface effects, testing, and communication.

Planned concepts:

1. a machine as a designed arrangement that helps perform a task;
2. inclined planes and the force-distance tradeoff;
3. wheels and axles;
4. levers and fulcrums;
5. pulleys, wedges, screws, and gears as bounded elementary mechanisms;
6. simple versus compound machines, task fit, safe testing, and revision.

Primary source: Core Knowledge Grade 1 Unit 4, *Simple Machines*. Advanced
mechanical advantage, torque, work, power, efficiency, structural ratings,
powered machinery, and safety-critical design remain later instruction.

### Group 13 - Living Things, Needs, Parts, and Survival

Moves from physical systems into living systems after observation,
classification, material, and cause-and-effect foundations are available.

Planned concepts:

1. living and nonliving classification with uncertain and formerly living
   cases kept explicit;
2. shared and differing needs of plants, animals, and humans;
3. habitats and environmental resources;
4. plant and animal parts related to functions and survival;
5. growth, adults, young, and introductory life-cycle patterns; and
6. organism-environment relationships, care, evidence, and bounded design.

Primary sources: Core Knowledge Kindergarten Unit 2, *Needs of Plants and
Animals*, and Grade 1 Unit 2, *Plant and Animal Survival*. Species-level detail,
genetics, evolution, diagnosis, and ecological policy remain later teaching.

### Group 14 - Weather, Seasons, Earth, Sun, Moon, and Sky Cycles

Connects observation over time, measurement, models, recurring patterns, maps,
and revisable prediction.

Planned concepts:

1. weather as observed atmospheric conditions rather than mood or climate;
2. bounded weather measurement and records;
3. recurring seasonal patterns with regional and hemispheric limits;
4. day and night as an Earth-Sun relationship;
5. observable Sun, Moon, and star patterns; and
6. prediction from recurring evidence with model and safety limits.

Primary sources: the selected Core Knowledge Kindergarten weather unit and
Grade 1 Unit 1, *Sun, Moon, and Stars*. Climate trends, severe-weather
operations, orbital calculation, astrophysics, and cosmology remain later
teaching. Direct unsafe solar observation is excluded.

### Group 15 - Human Body Systems, Care, and Health Evidence

Builds from Group 13's organism parts and functions while applying measurement,
cause-and-effect, source limits, and the distinction between knowledge and
medical authority.

Planned concepts:

1. the body as interacting parts and systems;
2. skeletal and muscular support and movement;
3. breathing and circulation as distinct connected processes;
4. digestion and obtaining usable materials from food;
5. senses and the nervous system as information pathways; and
6. ordinary care, health evidence, uncertainty, consent, and professional-help
   boundaries.

Primary source: Core Knowledge Grade 1 Unit 5, *Human Body Systems*, supplemented
where necessary by current authoritative public-health sources. This group is
general knowledge only: it does not diagnose, prescribe, replace clinicians, or
make an older curriculum source current by assertion.

### Group 16 - Helpful Computers, Information Tools, and F1 Integration

Closes F1 by connecting algorithms, symbols, components, inputs and outputs,
communication, evidence, tools, people, and bounded problem solving.

Planned concepts:

1. computers as human-designed tools rather than minds by default;
2. hardware, software, data, input, process, output, and storage distinctions;
3. programs and algorithms as instructions represented for execution;
4. networks and digital communication at a non-operational foundation level;
5. privacy, security, access, fallibility, and human responsibility; and
6. a cross-domain closure activity that uses approved F1 language, quantity,
   evidence, science, history, community, and design knowledge without adding
   hidden prerequisites.

Primary sources: Core Knowledge Grade 1 Unit 6, *Helpful Computers*, and the
already pinned Code.org Computer Science Fundamentals artifact. Procedural
knowledge grants no device, network, filesystem, code-execution, surveillance,
or autonomy authority.

## Residual Gap Ledger

These F1 requirements are audited during Groups 12-16 rather than assumed
complete:

- story versus informational text and questions of who, what, when, where,
  why, and how;
- naming unclear words or references and reconstructing short material;
- symmetry and recurring patterns;
- self, family, community, and institution as overlapping scales;
- needs, wants, goods, services, tools, components, inputs, and outputs; and
- cumulative transfer across subjects.

Small, already-supported omissions may receive source-linked bridge lessons in
the most relevant group. Any larger gap becomes an additional reviewable group.
The completion number is descriptive, not a quota.

## Teaching and Testing Boundary

Each group still requires its own source verification, implementation,
review-only preparation, and explicit Aleks authorization before retention.
Learning Evidence Activities remain descriptive and revisable. Unexpected or
incomplete evidence identifies the next teaching need; it does not grade Selene
as a failure.

Prefer source inspection, temporary databases, synthetic lifecycle checks, and
ordinary applications. Do not provoke fear, sensory discomfort, shame, or
adversarial behavior merely to test a lesson.
