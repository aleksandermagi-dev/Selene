## Lessons

1. factors, multiples, factor pairs, and exact divisibility (concept 226);
2. prime and composite classification through factor evidence (227);
3. justified base-ten divisibility patterns (228);
4. grouping symbols, operation priority, and left-to-right evaluation among
   equal-priority operations (229); and
5. multi-step expression modeling and independent verification (230).

Relationships come before fluency. The group distinguishes a factor from a
multiple, keeps one outside the prime/composite categories, explains that
divisibility tests are justified shortcuts rather than guesses, and checks
both a calculation and whether its expression models the intended situation.
